# Silver title disc

The homepage's existing `collaborations` slate now uses this scene. Existing title entries, uploads, order and valid project links are preserved. The opening intro and studio logo are unchanged.

- Portal → Title disc: 28 controls grouped into motion, projector/light, glow, artwork colour and slot appearance. The reference preset uses an upright diagonal pose and a prismatic fan; Cinema projector and Soft light fan are also available. Add blank slots, choose/upload artwork, remove slots and drag to reorder (up to 16 collection entries, with the first 8 on the disc). Resetting appearance keeps the images and order. Settings live in `proofTable.disc`; old documents receive safe defaults.
- The public scene loads near the viewport, sleeps off-screen or in hidden tabs, and provides Pause plus reduced-motion and non-WebGL views. Project links remain available in the keyboard-accessible collection list.
- `scene.ts` is shared with the standalone demo. Run `node scripts/build-disc-demo.mjs` after changing it or `lib/content/disc.ts`.
- Open `/disc-hero/index.html` for the single-file demo with lil-gui. Its `const images = [...]` uses embedded sample artwork; replace those URLs with your own. CDN modules are pinned. Remote images require CORS permission; failed images retain labelled placeholders.

## Rendering budget

DPR is capped at 2, with a separate 1.6-million-pixel framebuffer budget (800,000 on coarse-pointer / low-core devices). Sustained slow frames reduce resolution further. The renderer targets 60 fps on desktop and 30 fps on lower-power devices; actual performance depends on the device and other page effects.

Images decode sequentially, then downsample to power-of-two textures. For the maximum 8 visible slots, the combined base texture budget is at most 4 million pixels, plus mipmaps. One 128-pixel environment capture is generated at startup; there are no per-frame cube captures, shadows or extra blur scenes. Shared glass, geometry and mip-biased image sampling reduce work. Bloom and FXAA use bounded framebuffers. Observers, image requests, animation frames, materials, textures and targets are cleaned up on removal.

Separate rectangular glass cards leave silver visible between titles. Adding or removing a title redistributes the cards equally; changing size or spacing only rebuilds shared geometry, without loading images again. Each card displays only its uploaded title artwork, retains the source aspect ratio and has no generated labels, interior text or decorative placeholder shapes. Local card UVs keep that artwork rigidly attached while the disc rotates.

Each artwork fragment is transformed into the fixed projector window. Only overlapping pixels gain colour and clarity; the visible title ink receives a gentle pigment-derived emission that bloom softens around the lettering. The rectangular card and its frame receive no baseline glow. A quintic spatial feather eases the entering and exiting portions. Unfocused artwork defaults to 35% brightness and a moderate 1.5 mip bias so it remains recognizable. Saved custom brightness and blur values remain editable. Glow softness controls the window's feather.

The projector source and target stay fixed in world space while the disc spins. Its vertical entry point is measured from the real gap between the title and film description, rather than guessed from a viewport size. On stacked mobile layouts that point is safely constrained to the nearest stage edge. The source remains beyond the camera boundary at every responsive size, so the beam enters naturally from outside the screen instead of exposing its apex. Artwork outside the window stays grayscale; during a handoff only the overlapping portions of adjacent images are illuminated. A small 5×5 sample grid estimates each image's overlap and weights its pre-sampled average colour. The beam colour eases toward that blend over time, retaining its last colour through empty space. The beam remains continuously lit. This adds no per-frame image reads or render targets. Dispersion modulates the image colour instead of adding an unrelated rainbow. Blank production slots remain visually empty until artwork is uploaded.

The default landing area is on the left side (focus angle −180°), with the source remaining in the right-hand space. The light is a small marched volume inside one convex hull (6 steps and 7 rays; 4 and 5 on low-power devices), widening toward a fixed trapezoidal window fitted to the plaque and ending on the disc plane. Each ray fades in at a random spot, wanders, fades out and reappears elsewhere; the volume, dust motes and every surface read the same ray uniforms, so the light on the disc follows the beam. Frozen under reduced motion or a Ray motion of 0. Adding slots or changing their size automatically resizes the footprint; spread stays within the panel boundary. All three ray styles share this footprint. Saved custom angles remain editable; the portal's reference preset restores the left-side aim.

The website retains its shared ambient backdrop with a dark radial scrim behind the disc. The canvas clears transparently, so the disc and artwork remain solid while the surrounding background shows through; the standalone demo keeps its black background. The pinned Three.js bloom pass preserves transparent backgrounds without another blur pass or WebGL render target.

Material and post-processing references: [MeshPhysicalMaterial](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html), [UnrealBloomPass](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/UnrealBloomPass.html).
