# Silver title disc

The homepage's existing `collaborations` slate now uses this scene. Existing title entries, uploads, order and valid project links are preserved. The opening intro and studio logo are unchanged.

- Portal → Title disc: shared controls are grouped into motion, projector/light, glow, artwork colour and title appearance. Beam origin and landing controls are separate for desktop (1025px+), tablet (641–1024px) and mobile (up to 640px); cyan/gold calibration markers appear only in the portal preview and follow its current CSS width. Each title also has optional title colour, glow colour and glow intensity overrides. The reference preset uses an upright diagonal pose and a prismatic fan; Cinema projector and Soft light fan are also available. Add blank entries, choose/upload artwork, remove titles and drag to reorder (up to 16 collection entries, with the first 8 on the disc). Resetting appearance keeps the images and order. Settings live in `proofTable.disc`; old documents receive safe defaults.
- The public scene loads near the viewport, sleeps off-screen or in hidden tabs, and provides Pause plus reduced-motion and non-WebGL views. Project links remain available in the keyboard-accessible collection list.
- `scene.ts` is shared with the standalone demo. Run `node scripts/build-disc-demo.mjs` after changing it or `lib/content/disc.ts`.
- Open `/disc-hero/index.html` for the single-file demo with lil-gui. Its `const images = [...]` uses embedded sample artwork; replace those URLs with your own. CDN modules are pinned. Remote images require CORS permission; failed images retain labelled placeholders.

## Rendering budget

DPR is capped at 2, with a separate 1.6-million-pixel framebuffer budget (800,000 on coarse-pointer / low-core devices). Sustained slow frames reduce resolution further. The renderer targets 60 fps on desktop and 30 fps on lower-power devices; actual performance depends on the device and other page effects.

Portal adjustments to disc angle, beam aim, spread, light, artwork appearance and bloom share one time-based settling pass in the existing render loop. Slider updates therefore cannot create single-frame geometry or post-processing jumps. Reduced-motion mode applies stable values immediately. Control groups retain the user's own expanded state instead of reopening during preview updates.

Images decode sequentially, then downsample to power-of-two textures. For the maximum 8 visible titles, the combined base texture budget is at most 4 million pixels, plus mipmaps. One 128-pixel environment capture is generated at startup; there are no per-frame cube captures, shadows or extra blur scenes. Shared geometry and mip-biased image sampling reduce work. Bloom and FXAA use bounded framebuffers. Observers, image requests, animation frames, materials, textures and targets are cleaned up on removal.

Transparent uploaded title PNGs sit directly above the silver disc with no card, glass rectangle, border, generated label, interior text or placeholder shape. Adding or removing a title redistributes the invisible placement regions equally; changing size or spacing only rebuilds shared geometry, without loading images again. Corrected local UVs preserve the source aspect ratio and original reading direction while keeping artwork rigidly attached to the rotating disc.

Each artwork fragment is transformed into the fixed projector window. Only overlapping pixels gain colour and clarity; the visible title ink receives a gentle pigment-derived emission that bloom softens around the lettering. The window has bounded overscan so outer letters remain illuminated instead of fading at the placement boundary. The sharp artwork core is blended back over the emission and given stable focused opacity, preserving the uploaded logo's colour and detail when bloom is active. Transparent pixels emit nothing. A quintic spatial feather eases the entering and exiting portions. Unfocused artwork defaults to 35% brightness and a moderate 1.5 mip bias so it remains recognizable. Saved custom brightness and blur values remain editable. Glow softness controls the window's feather.

The projector source and target stay fixed in world space while the disc spins. On desktop its source is measured just beneath the final heading line rather than from an old hard-coded point. On stacked mobile layouts the horizontal source remains beyond the screen and the vertical point is safely constrained to the nearest stage edge. Artwork outside the window stays grayscale; during a handoff only the overlapping portions of adjacent images are illuminated. A bounded 8×8 pigment grid weights only the part of each title currently inside the light, so a half-entered title contributes colour only from that half. Colour eases in proportion to visible pigment; empty silver returns the beam to a neutral cool light instead of retaining the previous title. This adds no per-frame image reads or render targets. Dispersion modulates the title colour instead of adding an unrelated rainbow. Blank entries remain visually empty until artwork is uploaded.

The default landing area is on the configured side. The light is a small marched volume inside one convex hull (6 steps and 7 rays; 4 and 5 on low-power devices), widening gently toward the reflection centre and ending on the disc plane. Its coloured falloff is broader than its narrow neutral core, avoiding a thick white hotspot. Each ray fades in at a random spot, wanders, fades out and reappears elsewhere; the volume, dust motes and every surface read the same ray uniforms, so the light on the disc follows the beam. Frozen under reduced motion or a Ray motion of 0. Adding titles or changing their size automatically resizes the footprint. All three ray styles share this footprint. Saved custom angles remain editable; the portal's reference preset restores the configured aim.

The website retains its shared ambient backdrop with a dark radial scrim behind the disc. Wide desktop layouts offset the disc slightly farther into the open side of the composition, while narrow tablet and mobile layouts remain centred. The canvas clears transparently, so the disc and artwork remain solid while the surrounding background shows through; the standalone demo keeps its black background. The pinned Three.js bloom pass preserves transparent backgrounds without another blur pass or WebGL render target.

Material and post-processing references: [MeshPhysicalMaterial](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html), [UnrealBloomPass](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/UnrealBloomPass.html).
